About

Technology, strategy, and the uneven returns to innovation

My research connects innovation economics with strategy and international business. I use patent records, firm-level financial data, network analysis, and applied machine learning to examine technology spillovers, intellectual property, organizational adaptation, and the market value of R&D.

Much of my work compares China, Japan, and the United States, using differences in institutions and innovation systems to understand when technological investment creates private value and when it generates broader social returns.

Research

A comparative research agenda on technological capabilities

01

Innovation strategy

How the sequencing of exploration and exploitation shapes breakthrough invention, technological reorientation, and sustained firm performance.

02

Spillovers and intellectual property

How knowledge moves across firms and how patent depreciation, recognition networks, and intellectual property institutions affect private and social returns.

03

Global innovation systems

How firms build and value technological capabilities across China, Japan, and the United States, and how national environments alter the R&D-to-market-value relationship.

Panel econometrics · Patent analytics · Network analysis · Applied machine learning · Text analysis
